# Metaphor director

Choose one primary operation from the source relationship. Specify its geometry rather than its poetic intention alone.

## Unequal scale

Make a meaningful minor element large enough to become environment, burden, shelter, memory, or distance. Reduce the core subject only when vulnerability or persistence is central.

Specify the size contrast, overlap, and direction of pressure. Keep one identity cue so the metaphor remains source-derived.

## Occupied absence

Shape the empty paper like a missing subject, path, opening, or event. Arrange surrounding forms so the void becomes active and unavoidable.

Specify the void's contour, position, and what points toward or avoids it. Do not add arbitrary holes.

## Shared boundary

Join two subjects, locations, or time states along one contour. Let material, value, or edge distinguish the two sides while the shared edge shows dependence, conflict, substitution, or memory.

Specify which contour is shared and where it separates again. Avoid generic double exposure.

## Redirected current

Turn a source path, gaze, bridge, wind, reflection, queue, shoreline, or gesture into a current that bends, stalls, splits, or returns.

Specify how spacing, orientation, and density change along the current. Avoid decorative swooshes or motion trails.

## Material consequence

Transform one subject or region into a material that expresses what happens to it: stone becoming powder, water becoming thread, light becoming membrane, architecture folding into planes.

Specify where the mutation starts, how it progresses, and which identity cue survives.

## Time interval

Repeat one form two or three times with unequal spacing and controlled changes in completeness, value, material, or scale.

Specify whether the sequence approaches, recedes, hesitates, returns, or disappears. Avoid accidental duplicated faces.

## Counterweighted distance

Reduce the scene to two unequal masses separated by a measured interval. Use height, value, saturation, or directional opposition to express watchfulness, duty, separation, dependence, or imbalance.

Specify which mass enters first, which holds the gaze, and where the eye exits.

## Folded threshold

Turn a real boundary—door, shore, shadow, platform, ridge, window, or horizon—into a fold between states. Place the subject crossing, failing to cross, or held inside it.

Specify the fold direction, depth cue, and crossing point. Do not use it as a decorative frame.

## Combination rule

Use a second operation only if it performs a different role. For example, use `Counterweighted distance` to organize the composition and `Material consequence` to transform the hero. Remove the second operation if the proposition remains equally clear without it.
