# Wool Felt Memory Poster

Use the system `imagegen` skill and image-generation tool. This is an edit of the supplied travel photographs, not an unrelated illustration.

## Output contract

- Produce one independent raster poster per uploaded photo. Never combine different source photos into one image, collage, diptych, contact sheet, or shared canvas.
- Use a 3:4 portrait composition with two nearly equal-height zones separated by a clear, level, restrained horizontal boundary.
- Keep the upper zone as the clearly visible original photograph. Preserve subject identity and count, pose, orientation, architecture, terrain, core objects, camera viewpoint, relative positions, real materials, natural light, and original color atmosphere. Only apply subtle premium travel-magazine/exhibition color grading and very thin film grain. Do not redraw, stretch, mirror, warp, or alter the subject.
- Make the lower zone a three-dimensional fiber-art miniature of the same scene, staged on warm ivory felt paper. It must feel physically constructed from fibers, not like a photograph with yarn texture or a flat appliqué.

## Lower-half construction

Compress the source into one principal miniature landscape or diorama. Keep the most recognizable subjects, silhouettes, gestures, terrain structures, and narrative relationships; deliberately remove repetitive buildings, dense vegetation, tiny stones, and unrelated clutter. Change scale and spatial organization so it reads as a collectible hand-made memory model, small architectural maquette, fiber specimen, or tabletop landscape.

Use a coherent combination of `needle-felted wool`, `wool roving`, `bouclé yarn`, `crochet loops`, `embroidery thread`, `soft twisted fibers`, `small woven structures`, and `loose wool fibers`. Translate visible source elements into material-specific forms:

- clouds and mist: fluffy white wool roving with loose edges;
- mountains: layered needle-felted wool with varied density and direction;
- vegetation: bouclé loops, crochet knots, short yarn tufts, and sparse loose ends;
- water: horizontal blue-green yarns, embroidery thread, and curved reflective strands;
- buildings: compact felted blocks, miniature woven walls, and embroidered patterns;
- roads, shorelines, and terrain boundaries: stitched lines, cords, or low-pile structures;
- people, animals, boats, and vehicles: tiny fiber models retaining the source pose, direction, count, and dominant colors.

Show unmistakable fiber construction: fluffy wool, uneven loops, felt granules, yarn twist, embroidery stitches, slight fraying, loose threads, changing weave density, physical thickness, and soft contact shadows. Edges may be fuzzy and imperfect, but the subject silhouette must remain immediately recognizable. Derive and soften the palette from the source while preserving its key warm/cool relationship and signature colors; reduce visual noise and over-saturation through fiber color steps, loop density, and pile height.

## Quiet field and typography

Leave at least approximately 35% of the lower felt field as continuous, calm warm ivory negative space, excluding the miniature and text. Do not fill it with scattered yarn, icons, ornaments, or unrelated craft objects.

Add exactly three readable English text elements in the felt field:

1. a factual or image-derived 1–3 word main title based on the real place, subject, or action;
2. a small identifier in the form `WEAVE NO. 01`;
3. one factual 5–12 word observation sentence describing visible color, action, architecture, weather, or spatial relation.

The title must be formed from laid continuous yarn or hand embroidery with real fiber thickness, stitches, and slight looseness. Put the identifier and observation sentence on a small woven label, coarse cloth tag, or stitched marginal label. Keep all text clearly legible, physically integrated, and subordinate to the image. Never add smooth digital typography, author names, logos, URLs, QR codes, invented years, fake branding, or gibberish.

## Generation and review

Before generation, inspect each source and privately determine the hero subject, source-derived palette, simplest recognizable lower miniature, factual title, and factual observation sentence. Build a direct spatial prompt that includes the independent output rule, 3:4 ratio, upper photographic invariants, precise upper/lower split, lower fiber materials, negative-space requirement, text placement, and exclusions.

Review every result at full size and thumbnail size. If the lower half reads as a filter or flat sticker, strengthen physical fiber depth, construction, and contact shadows. If the upper half becomes illustrative, restore photographic detail. If the lower scene is busy, delete secondary details and restore continuous ivory space. If text is tiny, malformed, or digital-looking, simplify the wording and enlarge the stitched/label treatment while retaining factual content. Regenerate with one targeted correction when needed.

## Avoid

Missing source-photo comparison, flat felt appliqué, fabric stickers, flat paper cutouts, blanket-like yarn coverage, plastic toys, clay, resin, glossy CGI, smooth 3D rendering, generic craft templates, cartoon proportions, invented landmarks, changed subject counts, excessive decoration, unreadable text, floating digital typography, fake logos, watermarks, and multi-photo layouts.

Return only the independent edited images, one per source photo, with no promotional copy.
