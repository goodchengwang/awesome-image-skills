# Visual refinement director

Use this pass after choosing the collage architecture and before compiling the generation prompt. It prevents technically correct collages from feeling coarse, empty, or mechanically traced.

## Design the thumbnail silhouette

Reduce the planned artwork mentally to three values: photographic dark or mid-value, illustrated ink, and paper.

- The anchor and field should create one asymmetrical silhouette rather than two unrelated objects.
- Give the anchor one decisive visual edge or mass; avoid a tiny isolated hero surrounded by texture.
- Let the illustrated field noticeably change the outer silhouette or visual balance of the whole artwork.
- Avoid equal photo and illustration rectangles, centered snapshots, and uniform perimeter treatment.

## Control anchor authority

The photographic anchor needs one dominant identity cue and one attached support plane. Examples include a person plus bridge, building plus roof plane, boat plus waterline, face plus shoulder, or product plus cast shadow.

At thumbnail size, the anchor should be recognized before its seam texture. If not, enlarge the anchor, simplify the field near it, or increase local value separation.

At full size, the anchor must retain unmistakable photographic evidence: continuous-tone skin or stone, native fabric or surface texture, natural depth, realistic reflections, and source-like edge detail. Reject any plan that covers the anchor with paper fibers, halftone, line drawing, uniform fading, or print grain.

## Compose the field in three roles

Assign every illustrated mark to one role:

1. **Dominant mass:** the broad source-derived field that establishes scale and balance.
2. **Supporting gesture:** a path, horizon, branch direction, reflection, rhythm, or contour that leads the eye.
3. **Texture passage:** one local area of grain, dots, dry ink, or broken contour that enriches material without spreading everywhere.

Remove marks that perform none of these roles. Do not render every building, tree, window, wave, leaf, or roof merely because it appears in the photograph.

## Shape quiet paper

Quiet paper is an active shape, not unused background.

- Let it open in the direction of gaze, travel, light, weather, or the visual exit.
- Interrupt one large quiet region with a faint source-derived passage only when the region otherwise feels detached.
- Avoid broad empty rectangles with no axis leading into them.
- Keep the quiet region simpler and lighter than both the anchor and dominant mass.

## Refine the seam

- Reveal material transition only where it clarifies the handoff.
- Keep fibers, abrasion, or emulsion loss narrow and varied.
- Let one or two forms cross without a visible seam so photography and illustration interlock.
- Avoid a complete pale outline, equal roughness around the whole anchor, thick white tears, or deep cast shadows.
- Avoid a continuous top-to-bottom rupture line. Localize visible abrasion or migration around two to four structural contacts and keep the remaining boundary quiet.

The best seam is noticed after the anchor and the compositional field, not before them.

## Balance color and value

- Preserve natural local color in the photographic anchor.
- Preserve continuous photographic tones and native microdetail in the anchor; material texture belongs outside it.
- Translate the field into one principal ink family with no more than two neutral densities.
- Use a source-derived accent in one main location and, optionally, one much smaller echo.
- Check the composition in grayscale mentally: anchor, field, and paper must remain distinct without relying on hue.
- If the artwork feels faded, increase local value contrast rather than adding more colors or texture.

## Final elegance test

Revise the plan if any answer is no:

- Does the anchor have enough authority without becoming a pasted photograph?
- Is the anchor still unmistakably real photography rather than an illustrated likeness of the source?
- Does the field read as a designed mass instead of exhaustive tracing?
- Does quiet paper have a directional shape?
- Does the seam support the composition without becoming a thick border?
- Do anchor, field, and paper remain distinct at thumbnail size?
- Would removing the texture passage leave a strong composition underneath?
