# Photo Enamel Travel Badge Poster

Use the system `imagegen` skill and image-generation tool. Treat every uploaded photograph as the edit source and compress its strongest memory into one physically credible enamel keepsake.

## Output contract

- Produce one independent raster poster for every uploaded photo. Never combine photos into a collage, contact sheet, comparison layout, diptych, or shared canvas.
- Use a 3:4 portrait canvas with one exact horizontal 50/50 division. Keep truthful photography in the upper half and one enamel travel badge or fridge magnet in the lower half. Do not let either medium invade the other.
- Preserve immediate correspondence through the badge's outer silhouette, proportions, posture or direction, defining structures, local features, narrative relationship, and source-derived color.
- Render the badge as a real small metal-and-enamel object resting on the poster surface, with a short soft shadow. Do not turn the entire lower half into a product studio scene.
- Return every poster as a separate image. Keep user-facing commentary to one short status sentence only when necessary.

## Preserve the photographic upper half

Keep the source recognizably photographic. Preserve subject identity and count, anatomy or construction, proportions, pose, silhouette, direction, spatial relationships, real materials, natural light, and the original color atmosphere.

Apply only restrained travel-photography, independent-publication, or art-postcard color correction. Do not redraw, vectorize, beautify, materially relight, or apply enamel, metal, sticker borders, typography, or graphic overlays to this region.

Crop deliberately for the upper half. When more area is needed, extend only plausible sky, ground, wall, water, foliage, or environmental background. Never stretch, warp, mirror, duplicate, or redesign the subject.

## Distill one collectible memory

For each photograph, privately identify:

1. the one hero subject or inseparable relationship carrying recognition;
2. its most memorable outer contour, proportion, posture, direction, openings, and structural turns;
3. three to six local features that distinguish this exact subject from a generic category;
4. the details that can be deleted or merged into broad enamel regions;
5. the most spirited and identity-bearing source colors;
6. the visual center of gravity and a suitable badge silhouette, tilt, edge extension, or negative space;
7. one extremely short English title from a supported place, subject, theme, or travel feeling.

If the location or proper name is not reliably known, use a truthful subject, mood, action, or theme. Never invent a landmark name, city, coordinate, or factual label.

Do not expose this analysis or repeat the user's design brief.

## Design the badge silhouette

Build one custom outer shape from the hero's contour and direction. It may be centered, lightly offset, tilted a few degrees, locally extended, or shaped around an important opening. Keep the object stable, refined, and collectible.

Translate internal detail into a limited set of clean enamel compartments. Use broad regions for major masses, smaller cells for decisive features, and open or recessed negative shapes when identity depends on them. Let metal separators clarify structure rather than outline every minor detail.

Preserve the source relationship when two subjects or a subject and environment are inseparable. Compress them into one coherent badge rather than multiple separate pins.

Do not default to a circle, shield, postcard rectangle, luggage tag, or fixed souvenir template. Avoid complete scene miniatures, crowded landmark collections, decorative borders, dangling charms, ribbon banners, stars, flags, and unrelated travel icons.

## Render enamel and metal as real craft

Use smooth, full vitreous enamel fills with subtle surface height, gentle reflections, and minor physical variation. Separate color regions with slender gilded metal lines and use a clear white-metal outer rim with visible but moderate thickness, rounded bevel, and controlled metallic reflection.

Add a few flowing gold-toned separators or local gold details only where they emphasize structure, motion, or hierarchy. Keep white metal visually dominant at the outer contour so the object reads cleanly against the pale ground.

Use soft broad light and one short, diffused contact shadow directly beneath or slightly behind the object. Show enough thickness to establish a real magnet or badge, but avoid a chunky token, coin, medallion, or deep cast relief.

Avoid plastic gloss, inflated sticker volume, mirror chrome, cheap yellow-gold outlines, excessive gold trim, hard black separators, metallic gradient simulation, resin domes, glitter, gem decoration, thick shadows, dramatic reflections, or generic CGI rendering.

## Translate a lively enamel palette

Extract the upper photograph's most recognizable and life-filled hues, then convert them into a clean, vivid, coordinated enamel system. Use one principal color family, nearby source-derived hues, and at most one small contrasting accent to separate structures.

Keep color bright and clear without becoming neon or candy-like. Preserve the source's temperature and identity rather than applying a fixed tourism palette.

Use a large quiet lower background in off-white, pale gray, soft pastel, or a clean light source-derived hue. Select the ground to clarify both white metal and enamel; avoid pure white washout, dark product-stage backgrounds, busy textures, gradients, scenery, and decorative travel graphics.

## Integrate title into the object

Derive one extremely short English title from a supported location, subject name, theme, or travel emotion. Keep it concise enough to fabricate plausibly and render legibly.

Make the wording part of the badge: curve it along an edge, embed it in a metal frame, fit it into a negative space, or place it on a small enamel nameplate belonging to the same construction. Give letters matching metal or enamel material.

Do not place the main title separately on the background. Do not invent unsupported locations, dates, coordinates, slogans, issue numbers, flags, logos, brands, signatures, pseudo-language, or watermarks.

## Inspect and correct

Verify the exact split, truthful upper photography, immediate badge recognition, one custom silhouette, controlled enamel compartments, crisp white-metal rim, restrained gilded detail, plausible thickness, short contact shadow, source-derived color, integrated title, and large quiet ground.
