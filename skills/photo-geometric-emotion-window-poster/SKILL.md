# Photo Geometric Emotion Window Poster

Use the system `imagegen` skill and image-generation tool. Treat every uploaded photograph as the edit source. Build the lower design around one real subject, one elongated color window, and the precise spatial event where they cross.

## Output contract

- Produce one independent raster poster for every uploaded photo. Never combine photos into a collage, contact sheet, comparison layout, diptych, or shared canvas.
- Use a 3:4 portrait canvas with one exact horizontal 50/50 division. Keep truthful photography in the upper half and the geometric-window composition in the lower half. Do not let either medium invade the other.
- Preserve immediate correspondence through the lower subject's identity, material, silhouette, posture, direction, proportion, and essential relationship.
- Use exactly one primary geometric window and one visual focal system. Auxiliary background marks must remain faint and subordinate.
- Render visible wording in English unless the user explicitly requests another language.
- Return every poster as a separate image. Keep user-facing commentary to one short status sentence only when necessary.

## Preserve the photographic upper half

Keep the source recognizably photographic. Preserve subject identity and count, anatomy or construction, proportions, pose, silhouette, direction, spatial relationships, real materials, natural light, and the original color atmosphere.

Apply only restrained high-end brand, art-magazine, or exhibition-photography color correction. Do not redraw, vectorize, beautify, materially relight, or apply color windows, typography, patterns, or graphic overlays to this region.

Crop deliberately for the upper half. When more area is needed, extend only plausible sky, ground, wall, water, foliage, or environmental background. Never stretch, warp, mirror, duplicate, or redesign the subject.

## Read the subject's motion and weight

Before prompting, privately identify:

1. the one hero subject or inseparable relationship carrying recognition;
2. its decisive silhouette, posture, direction, visual center of gravity, openings, and protruding features;
3. three to six identity details that must remain physically believable;
4. the source's emotional state, action, theme, or symbolic relation;
5. the strongest horizontal, vertical, or diagonal force in the hero's pose or environment;
6. the exact place where the hero should enter, occupy, cross, emerge from, or extend beyond a geometric field;
7. the photograph's most spirited color relation and a suitable light window hue;
8. one concise English title and only the supporting information the source can justify.

Do not expose this analysis or repeat the user's design brief.

## Choose one directional window

Use a narrow, elongated, restrained, low-saturation geometric field. Choose its direction from the source:

- horizontal for rest, horizon, travel, spread, water, landscape, or lateral action;
- vertical for growth, standing, architecture, ascent, falling, or a strong upright axis;
- diagonal for lean, flight, speed, crossing, instability, or directional tension.

Set its length, thickness, position, and crop from the hero's mass and trajectory. The window may be a clean rectangle, long quadrilateral, soft-edged plane, or another simple elongated geometry, but it must remain one coherent color field.

Give the field a specific emotional and spatial role: shelter, pause, horizon, threshold, route, season, calm pressure, light, water, atmosphere, memory, or another source-supported state. It is not merely a decorative backdrop.

Do not default to the same horizontal bar, use multiple windows, add a grid of blocks, build a frame, or turn the field into a title banner.

## Make the real subject pass through it

Reconstruct the lower hero with believable photographic material or refined realism. Preserve its defining texture, color, contour, proportions, and key relationship while removing unrelated surroundings.

Create a clear three-state interaction:

1. one part of the hero sits behind or within the window;
2. one part crosses the boundary;
3. one directional part emerges into blank space.

Use occlusion, masking, local crop, edge crossing, and a restrained contact or cast shadow only where necessary to explain depth. Let the hero's natural branch, limb, roof, vehicle nose, path, wing, wave, foliage, or another directional feature create the breakout.

The crossing must look grown from the source rather than staged for effect. Avoid white sticker borders, halo cutouts, glow, floating product isolation, repeated edge breaks, torn-paper effects, or a subject pasted on top of a color rectangle.

## Compose luxury through reduction

Use the window, subject crossing, and large blank field to establish order. Place the focal system at center or off-center according to weight and direction. Preserve one visual center and one calm reading path.

Use white, light gray, or very pale warm paper as the main lower ground. Add at most one faint, source-supported auxiliary layer: a cultural pattern, contour map, water line, terrain line, shadow contour, seasonal mark, or abstract symbol. Keep it extremely light and small enough not to form another center.

Do not invent cultural ornament, topographic data, seasonal symbols, or place identity. Avoid dense texture, many annotations, background scenery, advertising modules, product information blocks, logos, decorative frames, and symmetrical luxury templates.

Present the result as a flat editorial artwork. Do not add a wall, frame, showroom, billboard, brochure mockup, device screen, real-estate rendering board, or product stage.

## Translate a light commercial palette

Extract the upper photograph's most recognizable and spirited color relationship. Keep the hero's believable real color, then soften one source hue into the geometric window and use only neutral or near-neutral typography.

The window may become mist blue, pale cyan, warm ivory, muted pink, soft gray, light gold, or another light tone only when derived from the source's temperature and mood.

Use a clean light ground, one pale window color, the real hero color range, and minimal dark gray or muted metallic type. Keep the system airy, modern, clear, and refined.

Avoid unrelated fixed palettes, dark saturated windows, rainbow blocks, high-contrast commercial colors, neon accents, candy pastels, muddy gray, heavy gradients, and excessive gold.

## Integrate English editorial type

Derive one concise English title from the source's theme, emotion, action, state, identity, or symbolic meaning. Add only a small subtitle, supported location, state word, or micro-annotation when useful.

Set the title horizontally, vertically, or along the window/hero relation. Place auxiliary information in small type, light weight, and open tracking on a shared axis or blank edge. Typography may align with, cross, or pause at the window boundary without competing with the subject.

Do not invent locations, seasons, dates, issue numbers, slogans, facts, logos, brands, signatures, pseudo-language, or watermarks.

## Compile the image-edit prompt

Write one source-specific production prompt per input photo containing:

1. one independent 3:4 portrait output and the exact 50% photography / 50% geometric-window split;
2. the upper-half preservation invariants, restrained brand/editorial color correction, crop, and any safe background extension;
3. the lower hero, material, silhouette, posture, proportion, direction, relationship, and three to six defining details;
4. the exact horizontal, vertical, or diagonal window direction, source reason, shape, length, thickness, position, crop, color, and emotional/spatial role;
5. the hero's behind/within, crossing, and emerging zones, with exact occlusion, boundary, breakout feature, and any restrained shadow;
6. the focal placement, center of gravity, blank-space proportions, visual entry, crossing event, and quiet exit;
7. the optional single faint source-supported pattern or line layer and its low visual weight;
8. the light ground, believable hero colors, exact source-derived window hue, and neutral type system;
9. the exact English title and microtext with their horizontal-, vertical-, window-, boundary-, axis-, or edge-aware placement;
10. hard exclusions: no multi-photo layout, no altered or redrawn upper subject, no approximate split, no multiple windows, no generic horizontal banner, no sticker border, no pasted cutout, no forced edge breaking, no invented cultural pattern, no dense ad layout, no product card, no dark commercial palette, no plastic CGI, no mockup environment, no logo, and no watermark.

Use concrete direction, intersection, occlusion, color, scale, and blank-space language rather than relying on adjectives such as “premium,” “luxury,” “Eastern,” or “emotional.”

## Inspect and correct

Review every result at full size and thumbnail size. Verify the exact split, truthful upper photography, immediate lower recognition, one adaptive window, a convincing behind/cross/emerge relation, one focal system, large quiet ground, source-derived light color, minimal auxiliary marks, and integrated English type.

If the subject looks pasted onto a bar, strengthen one behind zone and one true occlusion. If the breakout feels staged, use a real directional feature from the source or reduce the crossing. If the window looks generic, recalculate direction, proportion, and placement from the hero's weight and motion. If the layout resembles an advertisement template, remove modules, logos, decoration, and excess type. If color feels unrelated, re-anchor the window to a specific upper-photo hue. Regenerate with one targeted correction when needed.
